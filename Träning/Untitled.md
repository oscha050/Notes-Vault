# Program A — Heavy Strength & Load Handling

**Goal:** Build the ability to produce high force and handle heavy equipment.

**Time:** ~52–58 min including mobility  
**Frequency:** 1–2×/week

| Exercise                             | Sets × reps | Main quality        |
| ------------------------------------ | ----------: | ------------------- |
| Trap-bar deadlift                    |     3 × 4–6 | Max strength        |
| Overhead press                       |     3 × 5–8 | Upper-body strength |
| Weighted pull-up                     |     3 × 5–8 | Pulling strength    |
| Bulgarian split squat (Skier squats) | 2 × 6–8/leg | Unilateral strength |
| Farmer's carry                       | 3 × 30–40 m | Load carriage       |
## Exercises
### 1. Trap-bar deadlift

**3 × 4–6**  
**Rest: 2–3 min**

This is your main strength lift.

It trains the ability to generate force through the hips and legs while bracing the torso. That's highly transferable to lifting ammunition boxes, equipment, bags, people, or anything else that needs to be moved from the ground.

The trap bar is particularly useful because it allows heavy loading without making the movement unnecessarily technical.

**Intended effect:**  
**Maximal lower-body + posterior-chain strength and whole-body force production.**

Keep ~1–2 good reps in reserve. These aren't conditioning sets.

---

### 2. Standing overhead press

**3 × 5–8**  
**Rest: 2 min**

I prefer this to bench press for your priorities.

Military work involves moving and controlling equipment in front of and above you. The standing position also forces your trunk to stabilize while your arms produce force.

**Intended effect:**  
**Upper-body pushing strength + overhead stability + trunk bracing.**

Don't lean backwards to turn it into an incline press.

---

### 3. Weighted pull-up

**3 × 5–8**  
**Rest: 2 min**

This is your major upper-body pulling movement.

Pulling yourself over obstacles, climbing, controlling equipment and simply having a strong back all have obvious military applications.

Weighted pull-ups also give you excellent strength relative to bodyweight.

**Intended effect:**  
**Relative upper-body strength + pulling strength + grip.**

If you can't do 5 clean bodyweight reps, use bodyweight/assistance instead.

---

### 4. Bulgarian split squat

**2 × 6–8 / leg**  
**Rest: ~90 sec**

I deliberately keep this at only two sets because you've already done heavy bilateral strength work.

The point isn't to destroy your legs. It's to develop strength when one leg has to do most of the work.

That matters for:

- uneven terrain
- stepping over obstacles
- climbing
- running
- skiing
- carrying something while moving

**Intended effect:**  
**Unilateral leg strength + hip/knee control + balance.**

Use enough weight to make the final reps difficult without your technique deteriorating.

---

### 5. Farmer's carry

**3 × 30–40 m**  
**Rest: 60–90 sec**

This is where the gym work becomes very directly applicable.

Pick up two heavy objects and **walk**.

It trains your ability to:

- carry heavy equipment
- maintain posture under load
- brace your trunk while moving
- maintain grip
- stabilize your shoulders
- produce force while locomoting

**Intended effect:**  
**Loaded locomotion + grip + trunk/shoulder stability.**

Go heavy, but don't turn it into a drunken shuffle. If your posture collapses, the weight is too heavy.
